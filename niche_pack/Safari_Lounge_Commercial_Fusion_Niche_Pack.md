## Module A - Core Marinade and Sauce Bank

**Commercial function inside the venue:** This module creates the economic engine. Sauces and marinades convert low-cost base inventory into multiple high-perceived-value dishes. They reduce menu complexity, increase cross-utilisation, create upsells, and become future bottled or licensed IP.


### Safari Char-Grill Master Marinade

**Commercial position / venue role:** Primary protein marinade for chicken, beef, pork, goat, skewers, wings, and grilled tray specials.

**Standard batch yield:** 10 kg trimmed protein marinade load; approx. 22-28 portions depending cut.

| Ingredient | Metric quantity |
|---|---:|
| Light soy sauce | 900 ml |
| Fresh lime juice | 450 ml |
| Neutral oil | 650 ml |
| Brown sugar | 480 g |
| Fresh garlic, crushed | 280 g |
| Fresh ginger, grated | 220 g |
| Tomato paste | 350 g |
| Smoked paprika | 120 g |
| Ground coriander | 80 g |
| Ground cumin | 70 g |
| Black pepper | 55 g |
| Fine salt | 95 g |
| Water | 600 ml |

**Production method**

1. Blend all liquid ingredients with garlic, ginger, sugar, tomato paste, spices, and salt until smooth.
2. Pour over 10 kg trimmed protein in food-safe tubs and mix until fully coated.
3. Marinate chicken 6-18 hours, beef/goat/pork 12-24 hours.
4. Drain before grilling; reserve no used marinade for service.
5. Grill over high heat, then finish in oven if pieces are thick.

**Prep-ahead and holding notes:** Hold raw marinated proteins covered at 0-4 C. Label by protein and use within 24 hours for chicken or 48 hours for red meat.

**Reheating or service finish notes:** Finish with Gochu-BBQ Glaze or Coconut Curry Mop depending menu family.

**Packaging/plating notes:** Use black tray liners for BBQ drops, rice bowls, skewers, and catering trays. Garnish with lime, pickled onion, and herbs.

**Allergen notes:** Contains soy. Can be made gluten-free with tamari.

**Food cost logic:** High cross-utilisation lowers prep cost: one batch supports at least six menu items. Marinade cost should remain below 6-9% of finished item selling price.

**Menu pricing logic:** Proteins using this marinade can carry 68-75% gross margin when paired with rice, wraps, or fries.

**Cross-application opportunities:** Chicken skewers, beef bowls, goat wet fry, pork belly bites, family trays, bar snacks, bottled marinade pilot.

### Gochu-BBQ Sticky Glaze

**Commercial position / venue role:** Premium gloss sauce for grilled meats, wings, pork belly, brisket-style beef, and scarcity drops.

**Standard batch yield:** 5 litres glaze; coats approx. 18-24 kg cooked protein.

| Ingredient | Metric quantity |
|---|---:|
| Gochujang | 1.2 kg |
| Tomato ketchup | 1.4 kg |
| Honey | 900 g |
| Light soy sauce | 650 ml |
| Rice vinegar | 500 ml |
| Brown sugar | 650 g |
| Garlic puree | 220 g |
| Ginger puree | 150 g |
| Sesame oil | 120 ml |
| Water | 900 ml |

**Production method**

1. Combine all ingredients except sesame oil in a heavy pot.
2. Simmer gently for 18-22 minutes, whisking regularly until glossy.
3. Finish with sesame oil off heat.
4. Cool rapidly in shallow pans and store chilled.
5. Brush on proteins during final 3-5 minutes of cooking or toss hot cooked pieces in glaze.

**Prep-ahead and holding notes:** Keeps 7 days chilled. Hot-hold only the service amount; keep bulk chilled.

**Reheating or service finish notes:** Reheat gently with a splash of water. Do not boil hard or sugars may scorch.

**Packaging/plating notes:** Use visible glaze: lacquered skewers, sticky wings, shiny rice bowls, family tray drizzle.

**Allergen notes:** Contains soy, sesame, and chili. Check gochujang for wheat.

**Food cost logic:** Transforms standard proteins into premium visual products. Sweet-heat gloss increases perceived value with low added cost per portion.

**Menu pricing logic:** Use as a +$2 sauce upgrade, premium wing flavour, or Friday limited tray feature.

**Cross-application opportunities:** Wings, ribs, pork belly, beef bowls, loaded fries, bottled sauce, VIP drop.

**NotebookLM tags / future retrieval relevance:** sticky-glaze; gochujang; BBQ; premium-pricing; visual-abundance; scarcity-drop


### Coconut Curry Wet Fry Base

**Commercial position / venue role:** Batchable mother sauce for chicken, fish, goat, vegetables, beans, rice bowls, and family trays.

**Standard batch yield:** 8 litres base; enough for 32-40 wet-fry portions before protein addition.

| Ingredient | Metric quantity |
|---|---:|
| Neutral oil | 450 ml |
| Onion, fine dice | 2.2 kg |
| Garlic puree | 260 g |
| Ginger puree | 220 g |
| Tomato paste | 650 g |
| Crushed tomato | 2.5 kg |
| Curry powder | 240 g |
| Ground turmeric | 60 g |
| Ground cumin | 80 g |
| Ground coriander | 90 g |
| Coconut milk | 3.2 litres |
| Water or stock | 1.6 litres |
| Fine salt | 110 g |
| Lime juice | 180 ml |

**Production method**

1. Sweat onion in oil until soft and lightly golden.
2. Add garlic and ginger; cook 2 minutes.
3. Add tomato paste and spices; fry 4-5 minutes until oil separates.
4. Add crushed tomato, coconut milk, water, and salt.
5. Simmer 25 minutes, blend half if smoother texture is needed, then finish with lime.

**Prep-ahead and holding notes:** Blast chill in 2 litre containers. Shelf life 4 days chilled or 3 months frozen.

**Reheating or service finish notes:** Reheat base to 75 C before adding cooked protein or vegetables. Adjust thickness with stock.

**Packaging/plating notes:** Serve in black bowls, rice trays, or lidded delivery bowls. Garnish with herb oil and pickled chili.

**Allergen notes:** Contains coconut. Gluten-free if stock and curry powder are verified.

**Food cost logic:** Mother sauce supports high menu variety from one prep batch and protects margin through rice pairing.

**Menu pricing logic:** Price as comfort-premium: bowl, tray, or family pot. Use protein tiering for margin control.

**Cross-application opportunities:** Chicken curry bowl, coconut fish, goat wet fry, vegetable curry, curry fries, catering tray.

**NotebookLM tags / future retrieval relevance:** mother-sauce; coconut-curry; wet-fry-engine; batchable; delivery-friendly; low-inventory-variety


### Green Herb Lime Crema

**Commercial position / venue role:** Cold finishing sauce for wraps, grilled fish, rice bowls, bar snacks, and premium plating contrast.

**Standard batch yield:** 3 litres crema; approx. 150 x 20 ml portions.

| Ingredient | Metric quantity |
|---|---:|
| Greek yoghurt | 1.8 kg |
| Mayonnaise | 700 g |
| Fresh coriander | 220 g |
| Fresh parsley | 120 g |
| Lime juice | 300 ml |
| Garlic | 80 g |
| Jalapeno or green chili | 90 g |
| Fine salt | 45 g |
| Water | 250 ml |

**Production method**

1. Blend herbs, lime, garlic, chili, salt, and water until bright green.
2. Whisk into yoghurt and mayonnaise.
3. Pass through coarse sieve if using squeeze bottles.
4. Store in dated bottles under refrigeration.

**Prep-ahead and holding notes:** Keep chilled at 0-4 C. Use within 3 days for best colour.

**Reheating or service finish notes:** Serve cold only. Do not heat.

**Packaging/plating notes:** Use squeeze-bottle zigzag over bowls, wraps, fish, fries, and snack platters.

**Allergen notes:** Contains dairy and egg unless vegan mayo/yoghurt are used.

**Food cost logic:** Low portion cost with high perceived freshness. Helps justify premium pricing on fried or grilled items.

**Menu pricing logic:** Charge as included premium finish or +$1 extra sauce.

**Cross-application opportunities:** Wraps, fish tacos, loaded fries, rice bowls, skewers, staff meal upgrade.

## Module B - Pan Protein / Wet Fry Engine

**Commercial function inside the venue:** This module converts cooked or semi-cooked proteins into fast service items. Wet fry creates comfort, sauce gloss, aroma, and delivery durability. It also lets the venue run controlled protein batches and finish them to order.


### Base Wet Fry Aromatic Mix

**Commercial position / venue role:** Universal pan-finish base for beef, goat, chicken, mushrooms, and beans.

**Standard batch yield:** 6 kg aromatic base; enough for 60-75 portions.

| Ingredient | Metric quantity |
|---|---:|
| Neutral oil | 500 ml |
| Red onion, sliced | 3.5 kg |
| Green capsicum, sliced | 1.2 kg |
| Tomato, diced | 2.2 kg |
| Garlic puree | 260 g |
| Ginger puree | 180 g |
| Green chili, sliced | 120 g |
| Tomato paste | 450 g |
| Fine salt | 95 g |
| Black pepper | 45 g |

**Production method**

1. Heat oil in tilt pan or rondeau.
2. Cook onions until translucent with light colour.
3. Add capsicum, garlic, ginger, chili, and tomato paste; fry until aromatic.
4. Add tomato and seasoning; cook until thick and jammy.
5. Cool rapidly and portion into 600 g service packs.

**Prep-ahead and holding notes:** Hold chilled 3 days or freeze in packs. This is a base, not a finished sauce.

**Reheating or service finish notes:** Reheat 600 g base in pan, add 1 kg cooked protein, loosen with 150 ml stock, finish with sauce.

**Packaging/plating notes:** Visible onion and pepper strips signal abundance in bowls and trays.

**Allergen notes:** No major allergens unless stock additions contain allergens.

**Food cost logic:** Aromatic base adds perceived volume and reduces protein load per portion without making the dish feel cheap.

**Menu pricing logic:** Wet fry items should sell at 3.2-4x ingredient cost due to labour and aroma value.

**Cross-application opportunities:** Beef wet fry, goat wet fry, mushroom wet fry, breakfast bowls, loaded fries.

**NotebookLM tags / future retrieval relevance:** wet-fry-base; pan-engine; cross-utilisation; labour-control; abundance-illusion


### Black Pepper Beef Wet Fry

**Commercial position / venue role:** Fast premium beef pan protein for rice bowls, wraps, bar plates, and office trays.

**Standard batch yield:** 20 portions x 180 g finished beef mix.

| Ingredient | Metric quantity |

**Reheating or service finish notes:** Reheat in pan with splash of stock. Avoid microwave reheating for premium service.

**Packaging/plating notes:** Rice bowl: 180 g beef mix over 250 g rice. Tray: 2.5 kg beef mix over rice with pickles.

**Allergen notes:** Contains soy and sesame if glaze is used.

**Food cost logic:** Uses moderate beef volume with sauce and onion bulk. Premium perception comes from pepper aroma and gloss.

**Menu pricing logic:** Menu at premium bowl price; tray price should include labour and sauce premium.

**Cross-application opportunities:** Rice bowl, wrap, loaded fries, office tray, Friday beef drop.

## Module C - Fish or Signature Hero Protein Lane

**Commercial function inside the venue:** Hero proteins create brand memory. This module gives the venue a signature plate that can be photographed, run as a limited drop, and used for premium pricing while still relying on the same sauce bank and starch bases.


### Tamarind Coconut Fish Tray

**Commercial position / venue role:** Signature fish lane for premium rice trays, Friday specials, and lighter catering.

**Standard batch yield:** 18 portions x 180 g fish plus sauce.

| Ingredient | Metric quantity |

| Firm white fish fillets | 3.6 kg |
| Fine salt | 45 g |
| Lime juice | 180 ml |

| Coconut Curry Wet Fry Base | 2.8 litres |
| Tamarind paste | 220 g |
| Brown sugar | 160 g |
| Fresh tomato wedges | 900 g |
| Coriander | 150 g |

**Production method**

1. Season fish with salt, lime, and oil for 20 minutes.
2. Roast or pan-sear fish until just cooked.

**Packaging/plating notes:** Use shallow trays: rice base, fish portions, sauce, tomato, herb garnish.

**Food cost logic:** Fish has higher waste risk, so run as preorder or controlled Friday allocation.

### Safari Smoke-Rub Roast Beef

**Commercial position / venue role:** Hero roast protein for slicing into trays, wraps, bowls, and premium bar plates.

## Module D - Carbohydrate / Rice / Base Profit Absorbers

**Commercial function inside the venue:** Carbohydrates protect margin, carry sauces, create visual abundance, and make trays feel generous. They are the profit absorbers that allow premium proteins to be portion-controlled without making the customer feel shorted.


### Coconut Turmeric Rice Base

**Commercial position / venue role:** Default tray and bowl base for curry, fish, wet fry, and vegetarian systems.

**Standard batch yield:** 10 kg cooked rice; approx. 40 x 250 g portions.

| Ingredient | Metric quantity |
|---|---:|
| Long grain rice | 4.0 kg |
| Water | 5.2 litres |
| Coconut milk | 1.6 litres |
| Turmeric | 35 g |
| Fine salt | 75 g |
| Neutral oil | 120 ml |
| Bay leaves | 8 g |

**Production method**

1. Rinse rice until water runs mostly clear.
2. Combine water, coconut milk, turmeric, salt, oil, and bay.
3. Cook in rice cooker or covered gastronorm until tender.
4. Rest 15 minutes, fluff, and spread for safe cooling if not serving.

**Prep-ahead and holding notes:** Cook in two 5 kg cooked batches for easier cooling. Chill below 5 C within food-safety limits.

**Reheating or service finish notes:** Reheat with steam or microwave covered. Do not dry-fry reheated rice unless fully chilled first.

**Packaging/plating notes:** Use as yellow visual base in black bowls and family trays.

**Allergen notes:** Contains coconut.

**Food cost logic:** Low cost base supports high-margin protein and sauce sales.

**Menu pricing logic:** Price bowls/trays around protein; rice creates volume and perceived generosity.

**Cross-application opportunities:** Curry bowls, fish trays, wet fry trays, staff meal, vegetarian bowls.

# Commercial Control Appendices

These appendices turn the production recipes into a sellable hospitality system. They are designed for a chef-owner, consultant, operator, or NotebookLM user who needs to move from culinary ideas into execution, pricing, bundling, staff training, and repeat revenue.

## Appendix 1 - Venue Operating Model

The pack is built around a three-lane venue model:

| Lane | Function | Revenue Type | Prep Dependency | Customer Psychology |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daily Trade | bowls, wraps, snacks, sides | repeat cash flow | sauces, rice, pan proteins | low-thought yes, familiar comfort |
| Catering Trade | trays, office bundles, family packs | high-ticket orders | rice bases, proteins, sauces, packaging | group justification, abundance, convenience |
| Ritual Drops | weekly specials, WhatsApp preorders, VIP allocations | demand control | hero proteins, limited sauce batches | scarcity, visible allocation, urgency |

The strategic advantage comes from making these lanes share the same mise en place. A weak food business creates new prep for every channel. A strong one turns the same prep into different buying contexts. The customer sees variety; the kitchen sees repetition.

## Appendix 4 - Station Breakdown

### Sauce Station

Responsibilities:

- Produce marinade, glaze, crema, curry base, sauce flight portions.
- Label allergens and date codes.
- Maintain sauce bottle par levels.
- Record sauce usage against sales channels.

Critical controls:

- Never reuse raw-protein marinade as service sauce.
- Keep cold sauces below 5 C.
- Keep hot sauces above 63 C during service.
- Separate sesame/soy/dairy sauces for allergen clarity.

### Protein Station

Responsibilities:

- Trim and portion proteins.
- Marinate according to protein-specific time limits.
- Cook hero proteins and pan proteins.
- Track yield loss before and after cooking.

Critical controls:

- Chicken and fish require tighter batch planning than beef/goat.
- Hero proteins should create trim usage plans before production begins.
- High-crisp items should not be placed into delivery menus unless packaging protects texture.

### Base Station

Responsibilities:

- Cook rice bases, potato hash, flatbread crisps, and carrier systems.
- Protect margin through exact portion control.
- Build tray base layers quickly during catering assembly.

Critical controls:

- Rice cooling must follow food-safety rules.
- Potatoes must be held dry if crispness is part of the value.
- Carbohydrate portion creep destroys margin quietly.

### Assembly Station

Responsibilities:

- Build bowls, wraps, boxes, trays, and drop packages.
- Apply visual abundance: base first, protein visible, sauce gloss, garnish contrast.
- Check allergen cards and labels.

Critical controls:

- Sauce side-car for delivery unless gloss is central to the sell.
- Number allocation trays where scarcity is part of the ritual.
- Photograph real finished products for future demand signalling.

## Appendix 5 - Menu Engineering Logic

A dish earns menu space if it scores well across these commercial dimensions:

| Dimension | High Score Signal | Low Score Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Familiarity | customers understand it quickly | needs too much explanation |
| Novelty | sauce or format feels fresh | feels generic or copied |
| Batchability | can be cooked in controlled batches | only works one-by-one |
| Holding | survives delivery/hot hold | collapses after 10 minutes |
| Cross-utilisation | uses shared prep | needs unique inventory |
| Margin | base and sauce protect protein cost | protein-heavy and labour-heavy |
| Visual value | looks abundant and glossy | appears small or dry |
| Ritual potential | can be limited by true prep capacity | no reason to preorder |

Permanent menu items must satisfy familiarity, margin, and cross-utilisation. Weekly specials may carry more novelty, but they still need operational sense. Catering products must satisfy holding, packaging, and group justification.

## Appendix 11 - Allergen and Food Safety Control

Key allergen risks:

- Soy: marinades and glazes.
- Sesame: gochu glaze and Korean-style finishes.
- Dairy/egg: crema and mayonnaise-based sauces.
- Fish: fish trays and fish bites.
- Gluten: wraps, flatbreads, some gochujang, soy sauce.
- Coconut: curry base and coconut rice.

Food-safety controls:

- Raw marinade never becomes service sauce.
- Rice must be cooled rapidly and reheated thoroughly.
- Seafood should be cooked close to service and controlled by preorder.
- Cold sauces must remain chilled.
- Hot wet-fry items must be hot-held safely or cooled quickly.
- Fried/crispy items should not be sealed too early or texture will collapse.

Label requirements:

- Item name.
- Production date.

## Pricing Control Formula

Target menu price should be set by channel:

- Dine-in bar snacks: ingredient cost x 4.0 to 5.5.
- Lunch wraps/bowls: ingredient cost x 3.2 to 4.2.
- Family trays: ingredient cost x 3.0 to 3.8 plus packaging and preorder convenience.
- Catering: ingredient cost x 3.5 plus labour, delivery, equipment, and admin.
- Sauce retail: ingredient cost x 5.0+ after packaging and compliance.

## Operational Control Rules

1. Never launch a dish unless it reuses at least two existing components.
2. Every weekly special must produce either preorder data, sauce demand data, or catering lead data.
3. Every catering tray must be built from the same prep streams used by daily trade.
4. Every sauce must have at least three applications before it earns menu space.
5. Every hero protein must produce a secondary use for trim, leftovers, or controlled overproduction.
